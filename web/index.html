What you are looking at

A 3D model of the Mississippi Sound — Lake Borgne to Dauphin Island. The mainland waterline is the OpenStreetMap coastline, so the bays, bayous and river mouths are the real ones, and the islands follow their OpenStreetMap shorelines (Cat, West/East Ship, Horn, Petit Bois, Dauphin, Deer, Round). Depths are still a synthetic stand-in, not NOAA survey data. North is the mainland from Pearlington through Gulfport and Pascagoula to Bayou La Batre. Magenta ticks are the Louisiana and Alabama lines.

Moving around

Drag to pan east–west and north–south. Scroll to zoom. Right-drag (or two fingers) to tilt. Arrow keys pan once the map has focus.

Not a photograph

The surface is a heightfield. Each PNG tile stores depth in its RGB values (Mapzen Terrarium encoding). The GPU lifts every vertex from that depth. Bathymetry tints that surface by depth (hypsometric colour, hillshade, optional contours). Globe drapes Esri satellite imagery on the same heights so land and water both sit on the displaced mesh.

Zoom levels 6–12 are the same idea as a web map: more tiles, finer ground sample. They are not extra pictures of the same view.

This seed is synthetic

Tiles in this build are a procedural stand-in so the viewer runs with the network unplugged. They are not NOAA survey data and must not be presented as unaltered NOAA bathymetry. The coastline and island outlines are © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL).

Ocean overlay

On bathymetry, a snapshot HYCOM surface-current field can be drawn as particles. Motion is visually exaggerated and is not a time stepper. NDBC stations appear as wind barbs. This viewer is not an official NOAA product. Surface currents courtesy of the HYCOM consortium (dataset id when present). Buoy observations are NOAA NDBC open data.

Two views

Globe is CesiumJS on a WGS84 ellipsoid: Esri imagery draped on the same Terrarium height tiles. No Cesium ion token. Bathymetry is the custom three.js heightfield — hypsometric tint only; satellite stays on Globe.
